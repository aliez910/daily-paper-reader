## Abstract 

We present Magma , a foundation model that serves mul-timodal AI agentic tasks in both the digital and physical worlds. Magma is a significant extension of vision-language (VL) models in that it not only retains the VL understand-ing ability (verbal intelligence) of the latter, but is also equipped with the ability to ground and act in the visual-spatial world (spatial-temporal intelligence). To endow agentic capabilities for tasks ranging from UI navigation to robot manipulation, Magma is trained on large amounts of heterogeneous datasets that span from images, videos to robotics data, where actionable visual objects (e.g. click-able buttons in GUI) in images are labeled by Set-of-Mark (SoM) for action grounding, and object movements (e.g. trace of human hands or robotic arms) in videos are la-

creates new state-of-the-art results on UI navigation and robotic manipulation tasks, outperforming previous mod-els that are specifically tailored to these tasks. Moreover,

## 1. Introduction 

A long-standing research topic of AI is developing au-tonomous agents that can perceive visual stimuli, language

Recently, there has been a growing interest in developing AI agents based on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) mod-els [4, 5, 15, 21, 25, 32, 36]. These models are typically pretrained on large amounts of vision-language datasets and then action trajectories to attain the ability to take actions given VL input. However, due to the inherent difference between various environments ( e.g ., 2D digital world and 3D physical ones), VLA models are typically trained sepa-rately for simplicity and then used for different tasks. Ex-emplary models in the digital world include Pix2ACT [74] and Ferret-UI [91] for UI navigation, RT-2 [4] and Open-VLA [36] in the 3D physical world for robot manipulation. Although claimed as generalist, most of these models prior-itize learning a specific action policy at the cost of a signif-icant decline in generic multimodal understanding capabil-ities, rendering limited spectrum across tasks and domains. In this research, we strive to build a foundation model for multimodal AI agents. As depicted in Fig. 2, a capable multimodal agent should simultaneously possess two indis-pensable capabilities: • Multimodal Understanding to understand multimodal input from various domains (both digital and physical) not only semantically, but also spatially and temporally. • Multimodal Action Prediction to translate a task into an action sequence and execute the actions with precision in various environments. and such an agent system should be driven by external goals specified by human commands. Simply combining existing datasets from various do-mains does not bring benefits to the foundation model, due to the significant gap between multimodal understanding, which is mostly verbal ( i.e ., textual descriptions for images and videos) and action-taking tasks, which are mostly spa-tial ( i.e ., 2D coordinate for UI or 7-DoF for robot arm). To bridge the gap, we propose two surrogate tasks for model training, action grounding and action planning, by asking the model to predict the proximal action outputs given the visual-spatial observations, represented as images or video frames. Specifically, in each image, we label the visual ob-jects that are actionable by Set-of-Mark (SoM) (e.g., click-able buttons in Fig. 1 bottom middle) and object movements caused by actions in videos by Trace-of-Mark (ToM) (e.g., the trace of human hand or robotic arm in Fig. 1 top mid-dle). In this way, the images and videos that are not orig-inally associated with action labels, are transformed into “vision-language-action” data to morph the gap among dif-ferent types of tasks. We show through extensive empirical studies that SoM and ToM are environment-agnostic and easy to generalize to new agentic tasks, offering an effec-tive and efficient approach to scaling up our Magma model

pretraining using large amounts of labeled and unlabeled data, such as raw instructional videos. To the best of our knowledge, Magma is the first foun-dation model for multimodal AI agents that can understand multimodal inputs (see Fig. 1 left), perform action ground-ing and planning (see Fig. 1 middle), and undertake (un-seen) agentic tasks in both digital and physical environ-ments (see Fig. 1 right). We extensively evaluate Magma

In summary, our main contributions are: • We propose Magma , the first multimodal foundation model possessed of not only multimodal understanding but also action-taking abilities for agentic tasks in both digital and physical environments. • We propose SoM and ToM as surrogate training objec-tives to significantly improve action grounding and plan-ning, and allow Magma to effectively learn from large amounts of heterogeneous datasets. • We curate a large-scale pretraining dataset with a total size of 39M diverse samples, coming from not only VL datasets, but also UI, robotics data and human instruc-tional videos, all autolabeled using SoM and ToM. • We extensively evaluate Magma on a wide range of tasks. 

Magma with a single set of parameters achieves new SOTA on both UI navigation and robotic manipulation, and exhibits much stronger spatial understanding and temporal reasoning capabilities. 

## 2. Related Work 

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). Large Language Models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT [66], GPT-4 [67] and Llama [79] have demonstrated impressive reasoning and generalization abilities for text. The integration of visual in-puts into these models has led to a significant leap for mul-timodal research [68]. Building upon open-source LLMs [17, 79], a wide range of large multimodal models (LMMs) have achieved remarkable progress, led by pioneering mod-els such as LLaVA [51, 52] and MiniGPT-4 [99], which 14204 combine LLMs’ capabilities with a CLIP [70] based image encoder. Recently, a growing number of LMMs have been developed to handle a wider range of tasks and modalities, such as region-level LMMs [6, 10, 69, 92, 94], and video LMMs [48, 77, 96]. Another line of work focuses on re-ducing the cost of visual tokens [8, 61, 72, 95]. In parallel, more sophisticated benchmarks are proposed to assess these capabilities [7, 23, 24]. 

UI Agents in Digital World. There has been increasing interest in designing autonomous UI agents to navigate on digital devices. A canonical way is to exploit optical charac-ter recognition (OCR) and DOM parser to translate screen-shots into texts and then feed them into LLMs for action predictions [20, 71]. By nature, UI is visual, and thus an-other line of work attempts to train models to predict key-board and mouse actions in the pixel space. Some repre-sentative works include Pixel2Act [73], CogAgent [32] and SeeClick [15]. Instead of training the model end-to-end, a number of works prompt existing LMMs to perform tasks in the web domain [97] and mobile domain [81, 86]. These works typically convert DOM information or view hierar-chies to visual clues based on advanced prompting tech-niques like Set-of-Mark [88]. Our work is inspired by the promise of visual prompting, but instead train a model to recognize the visual prompts for action grounding. 

VLA Models for Robotics. Several studies have investi-gated the application of LMMs in robotics [5, 36, 46, 64, 90, 98, 100]. Among these, RT-2 [5] finetuned LMMs on robotic trajectory data, enabling the output of discretized robot action tokens. OpenVLA [36] is the first open-source VLA foundation built on top of the VLM backbone [35]. LLARVA [64] generated 2D visual traces for robot arms along with textual representations of actions, using visual trace prediction as an auxiliary task, while TraceVLA [98] used visual trace prompting to improve spatial-temporal awareness of robot policy. Most recently, learning from videos by predicting the latent VQVAE tokens is explored in [9, 90]. In this work, we follow a similar approach as OpenVLA to represent the action but leverage much richer multimodal data far beyond robotics datasets. Also, instead of asking model to predict latent tokens, we propose Trace-of-Mark as the targeted output to significantly enhance the spatial-temporal intelligence, which brings significant im-provements for robotics tasks. 

## 3. Multimodal Agentic Modeling 

3.1. Problem Definition 

A generalist multimodal AI agent should be performant for both multimodal understanding and action-taking. We de-fine a multimodal AI agent π, which takes past visual ob-servations I = {I1, ..., I k} and a task description task in text as input and generates outputs O as: 

Ol = π(I, task ) (1) where l ∈ { verbal , spatial } indicates verbal or spatial outputs. This formula generalizes across different tasks: • UI navigation in 2D screenshots . The task could be “book a hotel” and the output should include both seman-tic type of action ( e.g ., “type”, “click”, etc .) and the loca-tion (x, y ) or box (x, y, w, h ) where the action is applied. • Robot manipulation in the 3D world . For a task like “close the drawer”, the output consists of 6-DoF displace-ments (x, y, z, yaw, pitch, roll ) of the end effector and an extra dimension to indicate gripper openness. • Multimodal understanding tasks . For a regular task like VQA, the problem is reduced to a multimodal un-derstanding that generates a textual description and/or lo-cation of objects. The above formula poses some unique challenges: 

Pretraining objectives : Clearly, the above tasks have different focuses (verbal or spatial) and inherent domain gaps (UI, natural or robotics images). Moreover, UI nav-igation needs to predict 2D positions while robotics ma-nipulation generates proprioceptive actions which are not grounded on the 2D image observations. How to build a unified interface to facilitate joint training? Can we come up with surrogate tasks that can bridge the gap? 

Data scaling : Existing VLA data has a limited amount and diversity. On one hand, the largest robotics dataset Open X-Embodiment [65] consists of around 1M trajectories taken from merely 22 environments. On the other hand, UI data usually provides grounding annotations but not necessarily the actions. Videos, however, depict numerous human ac-tions and human-object interactions. Can we make use of these video data for our agentic pretraining? 

3.2. Method 

In this work, we propose a simple yet effective method to address the aforementioned challenges. Inspired by the gen-erality of Set-of-Mark (SoM) prompting [88] across UI nav-igation [39, 58, 86] and robot manipulation [33, 50], we use it to enable action grounding onto image observations for both UI and robotic tasks in that model faces much less difficulty in selecting the numeric marks than in predict-ing the original 2D and 3D outputs. We further extend it along the temporal axis and ask the model to predict Trace-of-Mark (ToM), which forces the model to learn a longer horizon by predicting distant future “actions”, and more im-portantly, provides an effective way to leverage unlabeled video data. 3.2.1. Set-of-Mark for Action Grounding 

SoM prompting [88] was first proposed to enhance the grounding capability of GPT-4V [68] and has been widely adopted for various agentic tasks [14, 33, 50, 63, 86]. Un-like previous works that used it to prompt off-the-shelf LMMs, here we propose to train an agentic model that takes advantage of it for action grounding, i.e ., locating action-able regions and further predict actions if needed. Given an image observation It ∈ R H×W ×3 at timestep 

t and a task task , we first extract a set of K candidate regions or points that are actionable: P = {p1, ..., p K },where pk could be a box or point coordinate. Subsequently, we overlay the marks and boxes (if any) to the correspond-ing location of the image with numerical labels, i.e ., M =

{1 : p1, 2 : p2, ..., K : pK }, giving us a new image IMt .Given the prompted image IMt , the model needs to pre-dict the action type and correct mark with coordinate. In this way, Eq. (1) is reformulated to: 

omark t = {action t : mark t} = π(IMt , task ) (2) where omark t is one or a few of marks from M.

3.3. Training 

We train Magma in an end-to-end manner. Once we obtain the SoM and ToM annotations for UI screenshots, videos and robotics data as depicted in Fig. 4, we integrate them into the original text and action annotations for a joint pre-training. We transform all annotations into language tokens to facilitate model learning. Specifically, we convert 2D ac-tions into a textual dictionary as in [15], and represent robot actions with the last 256 discrete language tokens in LLMs, following [36]. 4.2. Evaluating Agentic Capability 

We examine the effectiveness of Magma as the foundation model for multmodal AI agents on UI navigation, robotic manipulation, and generic multimodal understanding. 

4.2.1. Zero-Shot Evaluation 

We employ VisualWebBench (VWB) [56] and ScreenSpot [15] for evaluating UI action grounding and navigation, and SimplerEnv [45] for robot manipu-lation. In addition to these evaluations, we also validate our model on generic [28] and text-rich [75] VQA tasks as well as hallucination benchmark POPE [47]. As shown in Table 1, Magma consistently outperforms all other general-domain LMMs (e.g ., LLaVA, Qwen-VL) and domain-specific agentic models such as SeeClick [15] for UI navigation and OpenVLA [36] for robotic manipulation. It worth noting that the zero-shot performance of Magma 

on UI grounding even outperforms the SOTA method that uses GPT-4V ( 84.1 v.s. 71.6 on VWB), where both uses OmniParser [58] to generate SoM. For robotics tasks, we report the results on two commonly used simulator embodiments in SimplerEnv [45], Bridge and Google Robot covering 8 tasks with 172 visual matching and variant aggregation scenarios. Since previous methods used only robot trajectories for pretraining, the models lack generalization and are vulnerable to the real-to-sim gap. In contrast, our Magma model benefits from the proposed SoM and ToM technique and joint training on heteroge-neous datasets and achieves much higher success rates. In particular, Magma surpasses the 2nd place OpenVLA by 

20.8 %, nearly doubling the average success rate.

## 5. Conclusion 

In this work, we presented the foundation model Magma 

that can understand and act on multimodal inputs to com-plete agentic tasks in different environments. To bridge do-main gaps, we ask the model to predict Set-of-Mark (SoM) and Trace-of-Mark (ToM) as the surrogate tasks. Our exper-iments show that the use of SoM and ToM prediction tasks in pretraining significantly helps the model learn to ground and plan actions from heterogeneous datasets, particularly the human instructional videos. In our experiments, Magma 

shows strong spatial-temporal reasoning ability and signifi-cantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods on downstream UI navigation and robotic manipulation tasks. We hope that our work could help pave the way for the building of next-generation multimodal AI agents.
