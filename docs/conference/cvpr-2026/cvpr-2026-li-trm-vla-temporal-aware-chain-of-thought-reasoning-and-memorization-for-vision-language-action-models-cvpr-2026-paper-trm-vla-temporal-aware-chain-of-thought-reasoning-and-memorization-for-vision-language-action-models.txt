Abstract 

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a powerful paradigm for general robotic manipulation. How-ever, existing approaches typically omit intermediate rea-soning steps and directly regress actions, limiting reasoning interpretability and performance in long-horizon or compo-sitional tasks. Although recent studies introduce Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning into VLA models, their effective-ness remains suboptimal due to two key issues: (1) generat-ing a full reasoning trajectory at every timestep introduces substantial redundancy, thereby hinders real-time deploy-ment and (2) reasoning is performed independently, neglect-ing temporal consistency, which leads to planning conflicts. We propose TRM-VLA, a temporal-aware reasoning and memorization framework that integrates explicit temporal modeling into the VLA reasoning process. TRM-VLA con-sists of two core components: (1) Keyframe-Triggered Rea-soning (KTR), which identifies task progress and performs hierarchical CoT reasoning only at key decision points to reduce redundant inference; and (2) Granularity-adaptable Context Memory (GCM), which dynamically stores and re-trieves historical reasoning trajectories to maintain inter-frame coherence and global context. Extensive experiments on LIBERO-90, SIMPLER, and four real-world robotic tasks demonstrate that TRM-VLA achieves state-of-the-art perfor-mance while improving reasoning efficiency.

Ad-ditionally, several approaches mitigate knowledge erosion in VLA models through co-training on internet-scale language data and robot interaction data [37, 45–47, 56]. However, as shown in Figure 1(a)(b), current reasoning-augmented VLA methods generate a full and independent CoT at every timestep or at fixed intervals, without consider-ing the temporal structure of the task. This design leads to two critical issues: 1) Redundant reasoning . Most methods generate a full CoT trajectory at every timestep, even when adjacent frames share nearly identical visual and linguistic contexts. This results in repetitive reasoning, excessive CoT token generation, and substantial computational overhead with diminishing decision gains. 2) Independent inter-frame reasoning . Each CoT is produced in isolation, without tem-poral dependency or memory. The absence of historical reasoning causes contradictory action plans, breaking tem-poral coherence and degrading performance on long-horizon tasks. For instance, in sequential button pressing(e.g., “press red, then green, then blue”), static visuals prevent the agent from tracking which steps are completed, causing repetition and task failure. This non-Markovian dependency cannot be resolved by such frame-wise reasoning [39]. To address these limitations, we propose Temporal-aware Reasoning and Memorization for Vision-Language-Action models, TRM-VLA — a framework that introduces structured, keyframe-trigger reasoning and explicit context-aware memory mechanisms into the VLA pipeline. (1) Keyframe-Triggered Reasoning (KTR) . KTR performs hierarchical CoT reasoning only at key decision points (e.g., when the robot grasps the object) and is informed by task progress, thereby reducing redundant reasoning and token generation while preserving interpretability. (2) Granularity-adaptable Context Memory (GCM) . To overcome the lack of temporal consistency and memory, GCM maintains a dynamic memory buffer of past criti-cal reasoning steps, which are selectively retrieved through cross-attention to inform current decisions. By integrating historical reasoning into the policy state, GCM enforces tem-poral coherence, allowing the model to maintain consistent plans and to leverage memory to track task progress. As illustrated in Figure 1, unlike Pattern (a) [47, 50] (full CoT at every timestep) or Pattern (b) [10, 27] (CoT generated only at fixed intervals without memory), our approach com-bines hierarchy, keyframe-triggered reasoning with dynamic, multi-granularity memory. This breaks the implicit Marko-vian assumption of prior methods, endowing TRM-VLA with reasoning efficiency and temporal awareness essential for complex, real-world manipulation tasks. Our TRM-VLA framework builds upon a state-of-the-art multimodal foundation model [16, 18] and a Diffusion Transformer [22, 34] action policy. We adopt a dual-system architecture [6] inspired by cognitive science: the foundation model acts as System 2, responsible for slow, deliberate CoT generation, while the Diffusion Transformer [9] serves as System 1, executing fast, reflexive action prediction. The model is pretrained on the Open X-Embodiment dataset [32] and subsequently fine-tuned on task-specific robot interac-tion data for deployment. We annotate existing embodied datasets with multi-level temporal labels and employ causal attention with next-token prediction as training objective, en-abling the VLM to learn to implicitly identify task progress and generate reasoning at appropriate levels of abstraction, from strategic planning to concrete motor commands. Extensive experiments on LIBERO-90, SIMPLER, and four real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate that TRM-VLA not only achieves state-of-the-art performance (72.9% on SIMPLER [23]) but also reduces CoT token generation by 4× compared to existing reasoning-enabled VLA models. • We propose the TRM-VLA framework, which integrates temporal-aware reasoning and memorization to address the inference redundancy and inconsistency with frame-wise CoT generation for effective robotic manipulation. • We introduce keyframe-triggered reasoning (KTR) and granularity-adaptable context memory (GCM), which guarantee temporally-coherent planning by incorporating hierarchical CoT and dynamic memory retrieval. • We conduct comprehensive evaluation in real and simu-lated settings, demonstrating superior performance and efficiency across a diverse set of manipulation tasks. 

2. Related Works 

2.1. Generalist Robot Manipulation Policies 

Recent work has demonstrated that scaling robot manipula-tion policies across diverse environments and tasks signifi-cantly improves their multi-task and cross-scenario gener-alization capabilities [2, 17, 32, 43]. Training such general-ist policies typically requires scalable architectures capable of handling heterogeneous, large-scale, cross-embodiment robot interaction data. Vision-Language-Action models [14, 18, 22, 29, 30, 32, 36, 41, 45, 58] offer a promising paradigm: they build upon pre-trained Vision-Language Models and perform post-training on robot data. This approach effec-tively transfers the rich multimodal understanding of VLMs to embodied control, circumventing the data-hungry nature of training from scratch. Subsequent research has explored multiple directions to enhance VLA performance, including: Action generation architectures, such as Diffusion-based Action Experts for continuous control [4, 22, 45]; Dual-system designs that separate high-level reasoning from low-level execution [6, 40]; Multimodal data composition strate-10944 gies, incorporating tactile, 3D, or instruction-augmented inputs [13, 14, 47, 53, 56] and reinforcement learning fine-tuning to refine policies post-supervised training [12, 21, 49]. While chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning has substantially advanced LLM-based reasoning, its integration into VLA models has brought only marginal improvements. Crucially, most existing methods neglect the temporal structure of rea-soning inherent to robotic manipulation—where decisions are non-Markovian and depend on historical context beyond the current observation. In this work, we bridge this gap by introducing temporal-aware hierarchical reasoning with persistent memory as an intermediate representation prior to action generation, enabling temporally consistent decision-making in long-horizon and complex manipulation tasks. 

2.2. Reasoning in Vision-Language-Action Models 

Existing reasoning-enabled VLA approaches [8, 14, 50] can be broadly categorized into three lines of work: (1) Primitive-based reasoning. These methods use embodied-relevant primitives such as subgoals, trajectories, object bounding boxes, or keypoints as explicit reasoning intermedi-ates [11, 14, 20, 26, 52, 57]. By grounding reasoning in task-relevant visual or geometric cues, they improve policy focus and performance. (2) World model or video-generation-augmented reasoning. Some works employ world models or video generation models to produce goal-conditioned visual plans (e.g., goal images) that guide step-by-step ex-ecution [7, 52]. Others learn latent action representations from human demonstration videos and transfer them as pri-ors to robots [2, 48]. (3) Co-training with internet-scale and embodied data. To mitigate language forgetting during robot fine-tuning, several approaches jointly train on internet-scale multimodal data (e.g., image captions, VQA pairs) and robot interaction data [14, 37, 46, 47, 56]. In contrast, we pro-pose a temporal-aware reasoning and memory framework for VLAs: reasoning is triggered only at key decision points, and past reasoning traces are explicitly stored and retrieved to inform current decisions. This design better aligns with the memory-dependent nature of real-world robotic tasks.

We then detail the two core modules, Keyframe-Triggered Reasoning in Sec. 3.3 and Granularity-adaptable Context Memory in Sec. 3.4.

During training, the model is trained on embodied reasoning data annotated with multi-level keyframes and temporal stages, enabling it to learn when and how to reason. This diffusion-based decoding yields smooth, temporally coher-ent action sequences, crucial for stable real-world execution. 

3.3. Keyframe-Triggered Reasoning 

Our core insight is that effective reasoning in embodied tasks should “think appropriately” at each timestep, generating reasoning most relevant to the current scene (Eq. 3)—and to “think temporally” by integrating historical reasoning across time (Eq. 4). Together, these two capabilities strengthen the model’s temporal reasoning and allow it to perform embod-ied decision-making with long-horizon consistency. KTR achieves this by producing sparse, hierarchical CoT only at critical decision points, thereby aligning reasoning effort with task dynamics. As shown in Fig. 2, KTR is trained on embodied reasoning data augmented with fine-grained temporal annotations, as described below. 

Embodied Reasoning Annotation . We extend existing robot manipulation datasets with structured CoT annotations, following the ECoT protocol [50]. Each reasoning trace is decomposed into semantically distinct components: tag ∈{task, plan, perception, subtask reasoning, subtask, move reasoning, move, gripper position }, which collec-tively capture high-level task decomposition and low-level, perception-grounded action justification. At timestep t , this yields a structured label Rt = {rtag t }. To improve the qual-ity and temporal consistency of annotations, we introduce three key enhancements: (1) a temporal tracking model [15] for smooth gripper and object trajectory alignment; (2) a uni-fied VLM [3] for joint object detection, captioning, and rea-soning to ensure semantic coherence; and (3) an optimized annotation pipeline for improved inter-frame consistency and efficiency. Details can be found in the Appendix.

4. Experiments 

4.1. Datasets and Benchmarks 

SIMPLER Benchmark . We evaluate on SIMPLER [23], a high-fidelity simulation suite that closely matches real-world robot dynamics and visual appearance. A key feature of SIMPLER is its real-to-sim evaluation protocol: policies trained on real-world data are tested in simulation, with strong empirical correlation to physical-world performance under distribution shifts. This makes SIMPLER ideal for assessing reasoning-intensive methods, as it preserves the semantic and temporal structure of manipulation tasks while enabling scalable, reproducible evaluation. 

LIBERO Benchmark . We further test on LIBERO-90 [28], a challenging long-horizon benchmark comprising 90 language-conditioned tasks across 10 household domains. Tasks require fine-grained object interaction, multi-stage planning, and precise control—making it a rigorous testbed for temporal reasoning and memory. 

Real-World Benchmark . We collect 410 expert demon-stration trajectories on an AIRBOT Player robot across four carefully designed tasks that probe distinct reasoning capa-bilities: (1) Scoop Washers with Shovel : The robot uses a non-end-effector tool (shovel) to scoop small metal wash-ers, testing generalization to tool-based manipulation; (2) 

Clean Table : The robot clears diverse objects (bear, banana, cup, etc.) into a bin, evaluating long-horizon planning and object-agnostic execution; (3) Push Buttons in Order : The robot presses three colored buttons in a specified sequence, explicitly testing temporal memory and sequential reasoning; (4) Place Toy Bear into Box : The robot grasps a deformable object from varied poses and places it into a container, assess-ing robustness to object shape and pose variations. Together, these tasks span tool use, memory-dependent sequencing, sustained planning, and precise manipulation—core chal-lenges addressed by our framework. 

Embodied Reasoning Datasets . For the SIMPLER-WidowX benchmark, we build upon the BridgeData V2 [43] dataset and adopt the CoT annotations generated by ECoT [50]. For LIBERO-90 [28], we use the reasoning an-notations from ECoT-Lite [8], a streamlined variant of ECoT tailored to the LIBERO task suite. For real-world experi-ments, we employ the Qwen3-VL [3] multimodal language model to automatically produce embodied CoT labels from video-language-action sequences. Our annotation pipeline simplifies and consolidates the original protocol resulting in higher annotation quality and efficiency. 1

4.2. Quantitative Results 

Simulated Evaluation . We evaluate TRM-VLA on the SIMPLER-Bridge benchmark [23], which provides high-fidelity simulated environments. For each task, we report Grasp Rate (percentage of trials with successful object con-tact) and Success Rate (SR) (full task completion). As shown in Table 2, TRM-VLA achieves state-of-the-art per-formance across all tasks, significantly outperforming prior VLA and generalist policy models. Notably, TRM-VLA attains a 72.9% average success rate, surpassing the baseline method [22] by +21.6%. This substantial gain underscores the effectiveness of our temporal-aware reasoning and mem-ory framework in long-horizon manipulation. We further evaluate TRM-VLA on the LIBERO-90 bench-mark [28]. As shown in Table 1, TRM-VLA achieves a new state-of-the-art success rate of 94.8%, surpassing all prior methods by a significant margin. Notably, TRM-VLA outperforms the baseline model, CogACT-Base (88.4%), by +6.4%, and exceeds strong contemporaries like π0-Fast (83.1%) and UniACT (73.0%). This demonstrates that our framework excels at complex, multi-stage tasks requiring sustained planning and temporal awareness. 

Real-World Evaluation . On Push Buttons in Order, which demands explicit mem-ory of the button sequence, TRM-VLA achieves a 67% suc-cess rate, significantly surpassing CogACT-ECoT’s 45%. This gain underscores the effectiveness of our GCM module in preserving and leveraging historical reasoning traces. On Clean Table, a multi-step, long-horizon task involving object clearing, TRM-VLA attains a 72% success rate, compared to 41% for CogACT-ECoT. This highlights the benefit of our hierarchical, coarse-to-fine reasoning via KTR, which enables sustained planning over extended task horizons. Fur-thermore, TRM-VLA exhibits superior inference efficiency: it generates only 4.3 CoT tokens per step on average, a dra-matic reduction compared to ECoT (26.8). This reduces computation and mitigates context pollution, allowing the policy to focus on essential decision points. 4.3. Ablation Study 

Inference Efficiency . Since all compared models share the same backbone [16], generated CoT tokens per timestep serve as a direct proxy for computational cost. As shown in Tab. 1, our TRM-VLA achieves superior efficiency: it gener-ates only 4.3 tokens per step, a dramatic reduction compared to prior reasoning-augmented models. For instance, while ECoT (the reasoning version of OpenVLA) improves the success rate over its base model, it incurs a 3.8x increase in token generation (7.0 vs. 26.8). Similarly, CogACT-ECoT builds upon CogACT-Base but increases the tokens from 1.0 to 26.3. In contrast, TRM-VLA achieves a trade-off between performance and efficiency. This efficiency stems from our design principle of triggering reasoning only at critical frames, thereby avoiding redundant computation.

5. Conclusion 

We present TRM-VLA, a novel Vision-Language-Action framework that integrates temporal-aware reasoning and ex-plicit memory into robotic policy learning. By triggering hierarchical chain-of-thought reasoning only at critical deci-sion points and dynamically maintaining a structured mem-ory of past reasoning traces, TRM-VLA achieves strong per-formance on long-horizon, memory-intensive manipulation tasks while drastically reducing computational overhead. Ex-tensive experiments across simulated and real-world bench-marks demonstrate that our method sets new state-of-the-art results in both success rate and inference efficiency. We hope TRM-VLA will serve as a foundation for future work on temporally grounded, memory-augmented agents that reason strategically and act efficiently in complex environments. 10950 6.
