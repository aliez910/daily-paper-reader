# Abstract 

Robotic manipulation from visual observations remains challenging due to the lack of 3D consistent representa-tions that can generalize across diverse viewpoints and sensor configurations. Existing methods, primarily based on masked autoencoders or neural scene representations, struggle to capture robust view correspondences due to a lack of global 3D consistency or the requirement for scene specific optimization. Crucially, while multi-view diffu-sion models have recently shown tremendous success in 3D aware generative synthesis, their powerful representations offer a promising direction for achieving viewpoint robust visuomotor control. In this paper, we introduce DiffuView, a novel framework that learns unified 3D aware representa-tions through multi-view diffusion pretraining and deploys them for imitation learning. Specifically, DiffuView models the conditional generation of target views given source ob-servations within a diffusion framework, enabling the net-work to implicitly recover scene geometry and enforce view consistency. The pretrained diffusion network is then uti-lized as a powerful visual backbone for an action policy, allowing robust control under varying viewpoints and vi-sual conditions. We evaluate DiffuView on two challeng-ing benchmarks, MetaWorld and Libero. Extensive exper-iments in both simulation and realworld scenarios demon-strate that DiffuView achieves superior generalization, im-proving success rates under viewpoint shifts by nearly 20% compared with existing methods.

To this end, we propose DiffuView, a diffusion based framework for imitation learning that aims to acquire 3D consistent visual representations for robotic manipulation. Our method consists of two stages, as illustrated in Fig. 2. In the first stage, we utilize a multi-view diffusion model [4] as a starting point. This model is capable of recon-structing target views from source observations, having been pretrained on large-scale, web-crawled multi-view datasets including Objaverse [9], Co3Dv2 [40], Scannet [8], Real10k [63] and so on. Subsequently, we fine-tune the model on a curated robot centric dataset that integrates syn-thetic data with real world robot demonstrations. This pro-cess bridges the gap between general vision and embodied control, and encourage the pretrained model to learn uni-fied, 3D-aware representations through the capture of spa-tial correspondences across multiple viewpoints. Notably, while multi-view information is leveraged during training, the resulting model requires only a single-view observation during deployment. In the second stage, we integrate the pretrained model into an action diffusion policy network, where the learned representations serve as structured visual features to guide action generation. The policy is trained via imitation learning on robotic manipulation trajectories, re-sulting in robust visuomotor control that generalizes across varying camera poses and visual conditions. Overall, Dif-fuView demonstrates that enforcing 3D consistency in rep-resentation learning effectively enhances both the transfer-ability and generalization of diffusion based manipulation policies. To summarize, our contributions are as follows: • We propose DiffuView, a novel diffusion-based represen-tation learning framework for robotic manipulation that learns 3D consistent visual representations through multi-view diffusion pretraining and flexible view deployment. • We integrate the pretrained diffusion representation into an imitation learning policy, demonstrating that enforcing 3D consistency significantly enhances visuomotor robust-ness and generalization across varying camera poses and visual conditions. • We conduct extensive experiments in both simulation and realworld robotic manipulation scenarios, showing that DiffuView achieves superior visual robustness and trans-fer performance compared to existing approaches. 

# 2. Related Works 

## 2.1. Robotic representation learning 

Robotic representation learning builds on large scale visual foundation models. Generic self supervised objectives such as contrastive learning, self-distillation , mask autoencod-ing, diffusion models, together with language-image align-ment, produce strong 2D features that can be adapted to control [13, 17, 36, 39]. Building on these advances, a line of work learns robot-specific 2D embeddings from human videos or multitask manipulation data [22, 31, 33, 35, 52], but purely image-based encoders often lack 3d information. ## 2.2. Multi task action learning 

Multi task action learning, where a single agent acquires and executes a variety of tasks under a unified policy or framework, has long been studied in the robotics commu-nities. Recent advances in multi task action learning for robotics have progressed along three aspects: data scaling, architecture design, and representation grounding. On the data side, large cross-robot corpora enable generalist poli-cies that transfer across embodiments and tasks, including Open X-Embodiment [37], DROID [24] and so on. Ar-chitecturally, transformer based policies have become the most widely used backbone for unifying diverse observa-tions and behaviors [1, 3, 16, 46]. In parallel, diffusion based controllers demonstrate strong closed-loop perfor-mance and compositionality [6, 18, 42, 43]. A complemen-tary thread grounds multi task policies in vision-language-action (VLA) priors, leveraging internet-scale knowledge for transfer, as in RT-2 [65], Octo [34], and Pi0.5 [11]. Fur-thermore, 3D-aware representation learning improves spa-tial consistency for manipulation [15, 27, 28, 54, 58]. De-spite these advances, prevailing methods typically assume identical fixed camera configurations during both training and inference, which limits their ability to infer under new camera viewpoints. Our work targets these gaps by pretrain-ing view robust visual representations and enabling flexible view deployment within a unified multitask learner. 

## 2.3. Multi view generation 

Recent advances in Multi View Diffusion Model have sig-nificantly extended generative modeling from object level synthesis to complex scene level understanding. Early ap-proaches such as Free3D [60] and SV3D [49] explored gen-erating geometrically consistent novel views from a sin-gle image without relying on explicit 3D representations. Subsequent works including MV-Adapter [20] and 3D-Adapter [5] introduced geometry aware conditioning mech-anisms, enhancing cross view consistency and improving multi view alignment. Building upon these, models like CAT3D [14], and Seva [62] further scaled MVDMs to holistic scene generation, achieving improved spatial coher-ence and diversity. More recent efforts such as NVCom-poser [26] and Bolt3D [47] leverage 3D priors to enable rapid, high fidelity multi view synthesis at the scene level. In this work, we are the first to adapt multi-view diffu-sion models for pretraining view robust representations in 23603 robotic tasks, enabling flexible view deployment and bene-fit manipulation performance. 

# 3. Method 

Overview. In this section, we present our two stage frame-work for learning 3D consistent visuomotor in details. As illustrated in Fig. 2, DiffuView first pretrains a multi-view diffusion model to infer geometric correspondences across different camera views, enabling the encoder to capture cross view aligned representations. In the second stage, we utilize the pretrained perceptual foundation to extract features, which are aggregated and decoded by a diffusion-based policy network to produce continuous robot actions. Together, these stages allow the robot to learn spatially grounded, viewpoint robust policies for manipulation.

## 3.2. Policy Learning 

Visual Feature Extractor. After pretraining, we repurpose the diffusion UNet as a 3D aware vision encoder capable of extracting spatially consistent representations and per-forming implicit viewpoint transformation. Thanks to our flexible view inference design, the pretrained model can serve two complementary roles during downstream learn-ing: (i) as a feature extractor for action policy training, and 

(ii) as a viewpoint-adaptive encoder that enables visuomo-tor policies to reason from new viewpoints. Similar to the VPP [19], we employ the pretrained diffusion model pri-marily as a vision encoder rather than a denoiser, by per-forming only a single diffusion forward pass during infer-ence. Specifically, we extract multi scale features from the UNet upsampling layers, which preserve fine-grained geo-metric and semantic information. These features are then processed by a FiLM-conditioned Q-Former to obtain com-pact, task relevant visual embeddings. The FiLM condition-ing is guided by the end-of-text (EoT) token from a CLIP text encoder [39], ensuring that the extracted features are modulated by the semantic intent of the language instruc-tion. This design provides a unified interface between pre-trained visual representations and downstream action learn-ing, enabling robust visuomotor control. 

Diffusion Policy Network. After the FiLM conditioned Q-Former aggregates the visual features into a compact ob-servation embedding zobs , a diffusion policy is employed as the action head to generate the robot action a0 conditioned on zobs and the language instruction lemb . We also replace the standard Layer-Norm with RMSNorm for more stable training. In addition, we introduce an action causal self-attention mechanism to model temporal dependencies among consecutive action to-kens. Unlike vanilla self-attention, causal masking enforces an autoregressive constraint such that each action token can only attend to its preceding tokens. To further enhance ef-ficiency, we adopt a noise timestep conditioned Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) strategy [42]. At each timestep t, the router dynamically activates Top 2 experts within each MoE mod-ule on the current noise-level token η(σt), allowing the net-work to accelerate denoising. During inference, the model iteratively denoises a Gaussian sample to reconstruct the clean action sequence a0.

## 4.1. Pretraining Setups 

To enable our pretraining model to generalize effectively to the visual and geometric characteristics of robotic ma-nipulation scenes, we construct a pretraining dataset com-posed of diverse multi view observations tailored to ma-nipulation tasks. Specifically, we select a subset about 100 tasks of multi view sequences from the RH20T dataset [12], a large scale real world robotic dataset. Each episode con-tains proprioceptive data collected from multiple calibrated camera viewpoints using various robot arms and grippers. Since the original RH20T depth maps are often noisy and unreliable, we employ MapAnything [23] to estimate met-ric depth maps. Furthermore, the camera configurations in RH20T are relatively fixed, so we additionally generate di-verse robot centric RGB-D observations in simulation en-vironments. # 5. Conclusion and Limitation 

In this work, we proposed DiffuView, a two stage frame-work that learns 3D consistent visuomotor representations through multi-view diffusion pretraining and multi-task diffusion based policy learning. Our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple simulated manipulation benchmark. Furthermore, we evaluated the viewpoint generalization metrics on proposed MV-bench, confirming that our work can robustly handle large view-point shifts. These results highlight the effectiveness of our pretraining in providing a new paradigm for representation learning in robotic manipulation. Although our pretraining framework supports flexible view inference and enables viewpoint robust policy learning, it currently lacks an ex-plicit understanding of dynamic temporal information. This limits the model’s ability to reason about motion continuity dependencies.
